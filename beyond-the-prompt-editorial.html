01 — Purpose

What do Product Owners actually need AI to do?

Product Owners face constant pressure to ship faster, communicate clearly, and stay ahead of blockers — all while context-switching across tools, teams, and stakeholders.

This research set out to understand what POs actually need from AI, what tools exist today, and how well those tools address real pain points in the product management lifecycle.

Research Question

"How well do current agentic AI tools meet the evolving needs of Product Owners — and where do the critical gaps lie?"

08 — Limitations

But there's a ceiling

Copilot works well inside a single tool, within a known context. The moment a Product Owner's workflow spans multiple systems, sessions, or strategic decisions — Copilot starts to struggle.

Scope

Primarily restricted to internal Microsoft deployments (Teams, SharePoint). Cross-platform workflows are an afterthought, not a design principle.

Intelligence

Functions as a task bot, not a true AI agent. Excellent at summarisation and drafting — but cannot support complex, multi-step decision-making.

Language

Generalised language models struggle with domain-specific requirements. Ambiguous or conflicting product specs are handled poorly compared to specialised agents.

Memory

No persistent context across sessions. Each conversation starts fresh — creating the exact continuity gap POs feel most acutely in practice.

"

It's great for drafting my meeting notes — but it has no idea what we decided last sprint, or why. Every time, I'm starting from scratch.

— Theme from PO interviews

Our Assessment

Copilot is a strong productivity layer — but not a decision-support layer. That gap became the central question of this research.

10 — RAG Explained

What is RAG — and why does it matter?

Standard AI models are trained on general knowledge — they generate responses from what they've learned, but they don't know your product, your decisions, or your context.

Think of it as the difference between asking a knowledgeable stranger for product advice — versus asking a colleague who has read every Jira ticket, Confluence page, and sprint retro in your project.

RAG — Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Instead of relying purely on training data, a RAG system actively retrieves relevant information at the moment of a query — then uses that retrieved context to generate a grounded, specific response.

User Query

"Why did we deprioritise the notifications feature last sprint?"

Retrieval

AI fetches the relevant sprint notes, decision log, and Jira ticket history.

Grounded Response

"It was deprioritised due to a dependency on the auth module — flagged in sprint 14 retro."

11 — RAG in Context

Why RAG is the right lens for PO workflows

The difference between a generic AI response and a useful one comes down to context retrieval. The same prompt produces fundamentally different outputs depending on whether the tool can access your actual product data.

Query ✕ Without RAG ✓ With RAG
"Summarise our backlog priorities" Generic advice on backlog grooming best practices. Surfaces the top 5 tickets ranked by your team's actual scoring criteria, with blockers highlighted.
"Is this new feature aligned with our roadmap?" Generates a generic alignment checklist. No knowledge of your actual roadmap. Cross-references the feature against your current OKRs, previous decisions, and open dependencies.
"Draft release notes for this sprint" Template-style output. No reference to actual ticket outcomes or team decisions. Pulls from closed tickets, links to decisions made, and flags what was intentionally deferred.
For POs, context is the work. An AI that can't retrieve and connect that context isn't supporting product decisions — it's just generating text.
12 — Evaluation Framework

RAG maturity as our evaluation criterion

We realised early that evaluating tools purely on features — "does it draft user stories?" — missed the deeper question: how does it know what to draft? RAG maturity became our primary lens because it determines whether an AI can actually support PO decision-making — or just produce plausible-sounding text.

Level 1
No retrieval. Outputs based on training data only. Generic and context-free.
Level 2
Basic retrieval within a single tool ecosystem. Context-aware but silo-bound.
Level 3
Cross-tool, cross-session retrieval. Connects information across the full PO workflow stack.
The Evaluation Question

"When this tool generates an output — does it know your product, your decisions, and your context? Or is it just a well-dressed guess?"

Why This Matters

The tools that scored highest on RAG maturity were also the ones POs trusted most in real workflows — because the outputs felt grounded, not generic.

15 — Layer 1 Limitations

But workflow tools have a ceiling

Layer 1 tools are valuable — they fit into PO workflows with minimal friction. But they are ultimately bounded by the ecosystem they live in. Their intelligence is constrained to what that platform knows.

What if you needed to build an AI layer that could reach across your entire product stack — Jira, Confluence, Slack, CRM, support tickets — and retrieve context from all of it? That requires a fundamentally different level of RAG maturity.

That's the territory of Layer 2: agent and AI infrastructure platforms. Not plug-and-play tools — they require configuration and setup. But they expose capabilities that Layer 1 tools can't reach.

Layer 1 tools ask

"What can the AI do inside this app?"

Layer 2 tools ask

"What can the AI do when it has access to everything?"

Why This Matters for POs

The gap between what a PO needs and what current tools deliver is largely a RAG maturity gap. Understanding the infrastructure layer reveals what is technically possible — and what the next generation of PM tools should aspire to.

18 — Copilot Studio Deep-Dive

Where does Copilot Studio stand?

Microsoft Copilot Studio
Overview

Microsoft Copilot Studio distinguishes itself from competitors like Stack AI, Zapier AI Agents, Google Vertex Agent Builder, and Amazon Q by offering a deeply integrated, low-code environment within the Microsoft 365 ecosystem.

RAG Model Strength

Currently limited — Copilot Studio doesn't support out-of-the-box RAG-based grounding on web or open knowledge. It can retrieve from internal documents (like SharePoint), but lacks advanced document ranking, vector embeddings, or hybrid retrieval that tools like Stack AI or Vertex AI enable.

Price Point

Bundled with Microsoft Copilot licenses (M365 E3/E5 + Copilot license). Additional Power Platform costs for advanced workflows.

Jira Integrations

Achieved via Power Automate connectors, enabling calls to Jira API for retrieving/updating issues or transactional workflows.

Confluence Integrations

Via Power Automate HTTP connector, with ability to search pages or ingest content.

Knowledge Base Format Support
Supports SharePoint docs, Word, Excel, Power BI, Dataverse, etc. No direct web crawling.
Use-case Strengths
Enterprise operations, HR, IT service desks, finance, legal, project automation.
NLU (Natural Language Understanding)
Good for task-based prompts, but may not fully support open-ended or ambiguous queries.
19 — Conclusion

AI's current role in product workflows

Synthesising the evaluations across workflows reveals a clear boundary — not in what AI can do, but in what it understands.

Where it is useful
Supporting execution within workflows

AI tools consistently perform well when:

  • Drafting user stories, PRDs, and updates
  • Summarising feedback and discussions
  • Operating within a single tool's context

These capabilities make AI an effective productivity layer embedded within workflows.

Where it breaks down
Connecting context and supporting decisions

AI struggles when tasks require:

  • Persistent memory across sprints
  • Connecting information across tools (Jira, Confluence, Slack, CRM)
  • Translating feedback into strategic product decisions

These gaps stem from fragmented context, limited retrieval across systems, and a lack of grounding in real product history.

20 — Final Verdict

What this ultimately comes down to

The final verdict

"AI today operates within workflows — while product thinking operates across them."

What this ultimately comes down to

Across tools, the key differentiator was not capability — but how well AI retrieves and grounds its outputs in real product data. Systems with limited retrieval produce generic, context-free outputs. Those with stronger grounding generate responses tied to actual workflows and decisions.

This is not just a feature gap — it is a difference in RAG maturity.

The implication

Product Owners still carry the cognitive work of connecting fragmented context, interpreting feedback across sources, and making decisions. AI can assist in executing tasks, but it does not yet support how product thinking actually happens.

21 — Strategic Direction

Strategic direction

Our evaluation matrix reveals a critical market void: Product Owners are currently forced to choose between the seamless UX of Layer 1 tools (which lack deep project memory) and the robust RAG power of Layer 2 platforms (which lack out-of-the-box Jira/Confluence integration). To bridge this gap, future AI product development must focus on:

01
Unified Toolchain RAG

Moving beyond isolated chatbots to architectures that natively cross-reference Jira tickets, Confluence specs, and Slack decisions.

02
Context Over Generation

Shifting AI product value from "drafting text" to autonomously mapping dependencies and flagging sprint risks.

03
Turnkey Infrastructure

Transforming developer-heavy agent platforms into no-code, workflow-embedded solutions for business operations.

The UX Mandate

To gain true adoption, AI products must stop asking Product Owners to manage the AI itself. The real opportunity lies in invisible, system-level intelligence that connects the dots across fragmented workflows without ever needing a prompt.
