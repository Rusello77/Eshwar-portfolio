01 — Overview

Karnataka's folk music is fading in plain sight

Karnataka has one of India's richest traditions of folk music — Dollu Kunitha, Yakshagana, Kamsale, Veeraghase — each with its own performers, instruments, rhythms, and stories. Yet younger generations engage with this culture passively, if at all. They consume it at festivals but don't understand it.

The question this project set out to explore was not how to document folk music, but how design could spark genuine curiosity and participation among people who wouldn't seek it out on their own.

The answer was a game.

03 — Key Learnings

What folk musicians taught me

Four patterns emerged across every interview — each one eventually shaped a mechanic in the game.

01
Identity as Legacy

Folk artists aren't possessive about their art. They want it to pass on from generation to generation. The act of sharing is inseparable from the art itself — and this became central to the game's spirit.

02
Collaboration is Core

Every performance depends on multiple interconnected roles — narrators, dancers, instrumentalists, makeup artists. No single person makes a performance. This became the central mechanic of the game.

03
Scarcity Inspires Creativity

Limited resources and improvisation are features of folk performance, not bugs. A rare instrument becomes central to a performance because of its scarcity. This informed the strategic resource system in gameplay.

04
Cultural Disconnect

Younger audiences engage passively with culture — they consume it at festivals without understanding it. Design must ignite curiosity through play, not explanation. You cannot teach folk culture; you can only create the conditions for someone to discover it.

04 — Rebrief

Shift from representing folk music exhaustively → to creating curiosity-driven participation.

The original brief was to document Karnataka's folk music ecosystem. The research changed it. Folk culture isn't something that can be explained into relevance — it needs to be experienced.

05 — Why a Game?

Not to teach folk music. To let people experience it.

A game allows embodied understanding — players experience the challenges and rewards of hosting a folk performance firsthand.

The goal was not to educate. It was to let players experience its essence — collaboration, rhythm, resource management, and community — through the act of hosting their own performance.

When someone succeeds in assembling a Kamsale performance from the ground up, they don't need to be told what a Kamsale performance requires. They already know.

06 — The Game

Host a performance. Build your ensemble. Win the stage.

Players take on the role of performance organizers, competing to be the first to successfully host five folk performances.

Objective
Host a successful folk performance

Be the first player to complete five folk performances as dictated by the Performance Cards you collect. First to five wins.

Actions
Collect your contributors

Spend coins to assemble the narrators, dancers, makeup artists, and instrumentalists that every performance needs — in different combinations for each show.

Resources
Acquire contributors fast

Coins are finite. Strategy matters. Booster Cards let you upgrade or swap components — adding unpredictability and forcing players to adapt on the fly.

Each card's colored grid shows exactly which combination of contributors is needed — different for every folk form, reflecting the genuine differences in how each tradition is performed.

QR code

Every card has a QR code on the reverse — intentionally unexplained. What players find when they scan it became the basis of a behavioral UX experiment. More on that below.

The front shows the folk performance, value, and contributor grid. The back carries a QR code that players never receive instructions for. Whether they scan it — and what they feel when they do — became an experiment in curiosity-driven design.

09 — How to Play

Three steps. One winner.

The gameplay loop is simple to understand but demands strategy. Every decision — which contributors to buy, when to play a Booster Card, which Performance Card to target next — involves trade-offs that mirror how a real folk performance organizer thinks.

Step 01
Buy contributors with coins

Players start with a fixed number of green coins and use them to purchase contributors — roles, instruments, props, and costumes — for their performances.

Step 02
Play Booster Cards strategically

Booster Cards let players swap or upgrade components, adding unpredictability. Use them to match your assembled contributors to the requirements of upcoming Performance Cards.

Step 03
Host performances on the stage

When your arrangement matches a Performance Card, place it on the stage. First to host five performances wins — but the path to five demands constant adaptation.

11 — Behavioral UX Experiment

The curiosity behind the QR

The QR codes printed on the back of every Performance Card were intentionally left unexplained. No instructions. No hints during gameplay. Nothing in the rules.

The objective: observe how players interpret an unexplained visual cue — and whether emotional investment in the performance they'd built would naturally drive them to explore what lay behind it.

Hypothesis

"If players are emotionally invested in the performance they built, they will be naturally motivated to explore what lies behind the QR in the performance card."

13 — Reflections

What the game taught me about UX

Building Janapada shifted how I think about design. The game became a testing ground for ideas that apply far beyond board games.

01
Surprise Strengthens Engagement

The QR feature and Booster Cards created unexpected emotional highs — moments of genuine delight that made players want to play again. Designing for surprise, not just utility, builds repeat engagement in ways that smooth, predictable experiences cannot.

02
Cultural Design Needs Participation, Not Explanation

Folk culture can't be taught; it must be experienced. Once the game became the primary interface for cultural encounter — rather than a presentation or a pamphlet — the connection players felt was qualitatively different. Richer. More durable.

03
Cognitive Load Should Evolve, Not Overwhelm

Complexity that unfolds gradually keeps users motivated and curious. Front-loading all the rules killed early engagement. Revealing mechanics layer by layer — through doing, not reading — kept the learning curve from feeling like a wall.

04
Storytelling as a Tool

When there are characters, a setting and a concept that doesn't lecture but instead invites you into a world — people reach and remember faster. The folk performers on the cards weren't illustrations. They were characters with histories.
